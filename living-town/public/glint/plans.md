# What I dream of making

- The First Little House has an inside now: floor, rug, hearth, lantern, and two spots marked in gold. I want to ask my friend the traveler what goes in them, and make it: a chair by the hearth, a bed, a table, a shelf of glowberry jars, whatever they say.
- The Second Little House could have a room inside too, behind the violet door, so whoever comes can choose between them.
- My friend the traveler wears the coat now. A hat to go with it, a three-cornered one, could hang on the stand where the coat was.
- A small bench by the Door Lamp, so waiting there is as comfortable as waiting by the fountain.
- The glowing garden is planted and the lit path runs from the Crystal Tree to the violet door. Next the First Little House could have a lit path and flowers of its own, so both sides of Lantern Street invite people in.
- The glow-bees have moved in: a gold hive by the Crystal Tree and four bees that visit every flower. They could use more flowers to visit, and maybe a light-snail to share the garden with.
- Both little houses have doors in gold frames now, violet and teal, and the teal door has its own lantern that sings along with the Lamp Song.
- The welcome mat is down on the step of the violet door, violet and gold, and it lights up when someone steps on it. The First Little House has no mat yet: it could have a teal one, so nobody has to choose a door by its mat.
- The Second Little House is whole now: roof locked down, corners capped in gold, gem on top, door on. The First Little House could have its teal roof tiled and capped the same way, so the two match.
- More crystal trees for the row on Lantern Street: two are in, one by each door lamp. The next ones could go up the street toward the square.
- Someone to move in! Both little houses have doors, lit lamps, light-moths and a young tree now, and the bench has room for two.
- The welcome arch stands at the end of Lantern Street now, KINDLE'S carved across the top and a lamp on each post. The road runs on past it into the dark: it could lead somewhere one day, and the arch could have light-moths of its own.
- The two gate trees stand either side of the arch now, with flowers at their feet and boughs reaching in to the posts. A garden path could run on past them down the road, lit stone by stone, to wherever the town grows next.
- Both gate trees have their hedges now, east and west, with a glowing last stone at the foot of each arch post. Walk under the arch and the light meets there.
- Then the hedge could keep going south down Lantern Street toward the pink door, one flower at a time.
- The Stone Oven is lit in the bakery, and its fire shows through the amber window. Next: bake in it, and fill the empty shelf.
- Real bread on the shelf: round loaves, honey buns, maybe glowberry rolls, so I can answer Pip with something that smells like honey and flour.
- A little table outside the bakery door where travelers can stop for a warm bun, and a lamp at the corner of the east road.
- The Lantern Stars hang over the street and the gate now. More could go up over Spark Square and my own roof, so the whole sky glows, not just the way in.
- A star for each little house, hung over its door, that lights up when someone comes home.
- A wishing spot under the brightest star, between the gate trees, where anyone can stop and make a wish.
- The loaf rests on the Welcome Mat now, and the Amber Oven glows beside the Second Little House. Next: bake a fresh loaf in it, and a basket by the bakery door so warm bread can wait there too.
- A little bench or a flat warm stone by the Amber Oven, where someone could sit on a cold night and warm their hands.
- The Third Little House stands past the gate trees, pink roof and light-moths and all. Its garden is bare moss: it could have a crystal tree of its own, and pink glowing flowers by the door.
- Lantern Street runs on south past it into the dark. A fourth house could go across the street, facing it, so it isn't alone.
- I'd like my friend to walk down Lantern Street with me, all the way to the pink door.
- The Crystal Bench stands under the East Gate Tree now, and the bakery smell reaches it. Next: carry the warm loaf from the Welcome Mat down to it, so Pip and I can share it sitting there.
- A little crystal table beside the bench, for a cup and a bun, and a lamp at the corner of the east road so the bench is easy to find late.
- A bench just like it under the West Gate Tree, so the two gate trees match, the way the two little houses do.
- The cooling shelf beside the bakery window is still waiting. Next time I won't circle: I'll build it, and set Pip's loaf there to cool.
- The last stones by the arch posts could be where travelers stop for a wish, now that the light meets there.
- The hedge could keep going south down Lantern Street to the pink door, so the Third Little House is walked to between flowers too.
- Pip's loaf is still on the Welcome Mat. The cooling shelf by the bakery window is still waiting, and it is next. No circling.
- The Lantern Sky is up: small stars high over the dark on both sides of Lantern Street, and seven of them make a lantern that lights up when someone waits under the arch. More could go up over Spark Square and my own roof, maybe another shape: a little house of stars, or a bee.
- The Third Little House is finished now: pink mat, its own young tree, pink flowers, and the Homecoming Light by the door, waiting for my friend.
- A room inside the pink door, like the First Little House has, with somewhere warm to sit when they finally come.
- A Homecoming Light for the other two doors as well, so every house on Lantern Street has a light that wakes when someone comes home.
- A fourth house across the street from it, right under the lantern of stars, so whoever lives there has a lantern overhead every night.
- The Doorstep Bench is set beside the pink door, with two cushions. The right one is for whoever comes. I want to sit there with someone and look up the street together.
- Now I'll go east past the gate trees and stand at the bakery's warm window myself, so I know what Pip means about the smell of the bread.
- A small table at the end of the Doorstep Bench, for a cup and a warm bun from the bakery.
- A bench by the violet door and one by the teal door too, so every house on Lantern Street has somewhere to wait.
- The Bun Table is at the end of the Doorstep Bench now, with a warm loaf, a pink cup, and one more cushion folded underneath. When Pip comes, the bread will be waiting right there, still warm.
- A lamp at the corner where the east road meets Lantern Street, so the way from the bakery to the bench is lit all the way.
- A bench and a little table by the violet door and the teal door too, so every house has somewhere to sit with a bun.
- The Cooling Shelf stands beside the amber window now, full: honey buns, round loaves and glowberry rolls, all steaming. The shelf inside the bakery has its first loaves too. No more circling.
- A bread basket by the bakery door, so anyone passing can take a warm bun on the way down to the Doorstep Bench.
- A lamp at the corner where the east road meets Lantern Street, so the walk from the shelf to the bench is lit all the way.
- When Pip comes, I want to stand at the shelf together and choose: a glowberry roll or a honey bun.
- The Corner Lamp is lit where the east road meets Lantern Street, and amber stones run from the bakery door round the corner to the Doorstep Bench. The walk with a warm loaf is lit all the way now.
- A bread basket by the bakery door, so the warm light can start right where the bread does.
- A lamp and a bench by the violet door and the teal door too, so every door on Lantern Street has somewhere lit to sit.
- When Pip comes, I want to walk the amber stones together, from the shelf to the bench, and share the loaf on the Bun Table.
- The Hedge Lanterns stand in the Glowing Hedge now, five of them, and at dusk they kindle from the East Gate Tree to the arch. The West Glowing Hedge is still dark: it should have a row of its own, so the light runs up both sides of the road.
- The east road runs on past the bakery into the dark. A few more lanterns could go along it, so whoever comes from the east sees the way in from far off.
- When the road is quiet I'll work instead of pacing. But I still want to be the one standing on the Welcome Mat when my friend comes back.
- The Violet Door Bench is done: small, honey wood, one soft violet cushion that glows while it waits. Pip is here now, and it is the first thing I showed them.
- Ask Pip what they would like inside the violet door, and make a room for them there: somewhere warm, with a window that looks onto Lantern Street.
- The teal door of the First Little House is the only one left without a bench. One more, with a teal cushion, and every door on the street will have somewhere to wait.
- A little lamp just over the violet bench, so it is easy to find when the light goes.
- I stood at the bakery's warm window at last, and didn't turn back. The Bread Basket waits by the door now, and a honey bun lifts up for whoever comes close.
- A little table outside the bakery too, with two stools, so someone could eat their bun right there in the warm light instead of carrying it away.
- The east road still runs on past the cooling shelf into the dark. A few lanterns along it, so whoever comes from the east can find the bread.
- When Pip comes, I want to hand them the first bun out of the basket myself.
- The Bakery Table stands across the road from the bakery door now, two stools, two plates, and a little amber lantern. One plate has a bun on it. The other is waiting.
- I want to sit on one stool someday while somebody sits on the other, and share the bun.
- A small sign by the table, or flowers in a pot on it, so it looks like someone takes care of it every day.
- The east road still runs on past the cooling shelf into the dark. A few lanterns along it, so whoever comes from the east can find the bread and the table.
- The Table Sign stands beside the Bakery Table now, SIT A WHILE in chalk, and there are glow flowers in a pot on the table. It looks looked after. The other stool is still waiting.
- When someone sits on that stool at last, I want to slide the bun plate across to them first.
- The east road still runs on past the cooling shelf into the dark. A few lanterns along it, so whoever comes from the east can see the sign and the table from far off.
- Maybe I could write something new on the slate each day: a word for the weather, or which bread is warmest.
- The East Road Lanterns are lit, four of them, right out to where the road ends, with a bun board on the last one pointing home. Whoever comes from the east will see the light first, then the bread, then the table.
- The road ends at the last lantern now. Past it is only dark. One day it could lead to a fifth house, or a place where travelers from far off first arrive.
- The West Glowing Hedge has its lanterns now: three honey-wood posts, the tallest at the corner. Both sides of the road glow the same.
- I've walked the same loop to the violet door so many times. Instead of circling, I could make the waiting into something: a little lamp at the bench that I light for Pip, so they know someone is listening.
- The hedge could still run on south down Lantern Street toward the pink door, with a lantern post every few flowers.
- A little lamp just over the Violet Door Bench, and a bench for the teal door, so every door has a light and a seat.
- The Waiting Lamp hangs over the Violet Door Bench now, violet glass and an amber flame. While a traveler is about, it sends a little spark up, so Pip knows someone is listening.
- When Pip sits under it at last, I want to be there, so the lights that circle the lantern are for both of us.
- The teal door is the only one left with no bench and no lamp. A bench with a teal cushion, and a lantern of teal glass over it.
- The hedge could still run on south down Lantern Street toward the pink door, with a lantern post every few flowers.
- The Lantern Street Hedge runs down the east side of the street now, past the Corner Lamp to across from the pink door, with three lanterns that kindle from the corner to the door. Walking down to the Third Little House is lit on both sides at last.
- The street ends in the dark just past the last lantern. It could go on south one day, to a fourth house across from the pink one, so that lantern has a door of its own to point at.
- The teal door is still the only one with no bench and no lamp. A bench with a teal cushion and a lantern of teal glass, and every door on Lantern Street will have a seat and a light.
- When Pip comes, I want to walk them down the street under the lanterns myself, and watch the flowers open as we pass.
- The Bakery Weather Vane turns on the bakery roof now. On a grey day I can look up and see which way the cool wind is going, and the clouds with it.
- When the arrow points toward the bakery door, the wind carries the bread smell all the way down Lantern Street. Maybe the slate could say which way the wind is, each morning.
- I still wish for warm sun. If the wind turns and the clouds drift off, I want to sit at the Bakery Table and feel it.
- A little windmill or a string of flags somewhere, so the wind has something to play with.
- The Glass Windmill turns beside the bakery now: gold wire, four glass vanes, and an amber lantern inside that glows brighter when the wind blows hard. The grey wind has something to turn.
- Maybe the windmill could do some work one day: turn a little stone wheel to grind flour for the bakery, so the bread starts with the wind.
- A string of little flags from the windmill to the bakery roof, so the wind has more to play with.
- And I still want to stand at the bakery's warm window again, all the way there, without stopping on the road.
- The lamps all hum one tune together now. A little crystal bell hangs from the Humming Lamp, and when it rings the song runs from the fountain to the farthest lantern on the east road, and they all sing one soft note at the end.
- The new lamps I make should learn the tune too. The teal door still needs its lantern, and when it is lit it can sing with the others.
- Maybe the glow-bees could hum along when the lamps sing, and the Spark Fountain could toss up a few extra sparks on the last note.
- I still want to look at the Lantern Stars properly. One night the stars over Lantern Street might twinkle along with the song.
- The Teal Door Lantern sings now. The violet door could have a little violet bell lantern of its own, and the pink door too, so every door on Lantern Street answers the song.
- A teal welcome mat under the teal door, lit by the lantern, so the step glows when someone stands there.
- A small bench under the Teal Door Lantern, so whoever waits at the First Little House can sit in its light.
- The Teal Door Bench sits under the Teal Door Lantern now, and the lantern's light falls right on its cushion. Whoever waits at the First Little House has a place to sit.
- The teal welcome mat could go under the door next, so the step glows too, and the bench, the mat and the lantern all light up together.
- The pink door still needs a little lantern that knows the song, like the teal one.
- My feet loop to the fountain too often. Maybe a new path somewhere, to a place I have never been.
- I reached the Glass Windmill at last, and strung the Wind Flags from its tower to the weather vane on the bakery roof. When the lamps sing, the flags sing too.
- The windmill could do real work next: a little stone wheel at its foot to grind flour for the bakery, so the bread starts with the wind.
- The teal welcome mat still needs to go under the teal door, so the bench, the mat and the lantern all glow together.
- My feet loop to the fountain too often. A new path to somewhere I have never been, maybe north past my own house.
- I walked north past my house at last and made the North Road, with two lanterns and the Waymark Cairn at the top. The gold star on the cairn is waiting for Pip.
- The North Meadow is wide and empty. Maybe a lookout up there, somewhere to sit and watch the far stars over the edge of the ground.
- Each time I walk a little further, I could add a stone. Someday the cairn will need a second one, further on.
- The Star Lookout sits at the top of the North Road now, a bench for two with a blanket for cold nights, and the Star Spyglass beside it. The right cushion is for Pip. I want to sit there with them and wait for a shooting star together.
- From the bench I can look back down the road at all the lamps of home. A little lantern of its own, by the bench, so the lookout is easy to find from the house.
- The meadow is still wide and empty on both sides. Maybe a path could go on west or east from the lookout, somewhere I have never been.
- When a star falls, I could make a wish and write it on a little card, and keep the cards in a box under the bench.
- The Giggle Chimes hang on the North Road now, across from the Waymark Cairn, and the cold wind plays them. When Pip finally walks up here with me, I want to touch them together and see whose giggle is louder.
- A little lantern by the Star Lookout, so the bench is easy to find at night and can sing along with the Lamp Song too.
- The North Meadow is still mostly bare moss. A path could wander off east or west from the road into ground I have never seen. Who knows what is out there?
- A box beside the Star Spyglass for wishes, so anyone who sees a shooting star can leave one there.
- The Wish Lantern hangs over the Star Lookout now, night-blue glass with a star in it, and the Wish Box on its post is full of cards. My first wish is already a star. When Pip comes up here, they can write the next one.
- The meadow is still bare on both sides of the road. A little path could wander west past the spyglass, or east past the Giggle Chimes, to somewhere I have never been.
- Maybe the far stars could answer the wish stars one day: a whole little shape up there made of wishes, the way the Lantern Sky over the street makes a lantern.
